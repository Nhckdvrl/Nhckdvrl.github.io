Why I wanted a research homepage that doesn't look like a CV.

A placeholder first post, mostly to make sure the blog has somewhere to grow.

Most academic homepages are useful, but they are often organized like a compressed CV: affiliation, publications, awards, links. That is good for lookup, but not always good for showing what someone is actually thinking about.

I want this site to do both. The formal parts can live on separate pages; the blog can be messier. It can hold research questions before they become papers, notes after experiments fail, engineering details that do not belong in a methods section, and occasional things that have nothing to do with research.

The rule

If something is useful to remember but too informal for a paper, it can probably live here.

A research homepage should show not only what was finished, but what is currently alive.

This post is intentionally short. It is here so the structure exists; future notes can replace it with something more interesting.
